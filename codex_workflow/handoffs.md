# Bounded assignments, useful context reuse and native lifecycle

Use at substantive scope, duty, risk or ownership changes, not each tool call. Retain suitable
context for related corrections and accepted decisions. A long thread can contain many legitimate
assignments; its original role label is not a measurement of all subsequent work.

## Transfer the assignment, not just another message

A compact assignment states: task/requirement, deliverable, native owner/preset, purpose and risk,
read/write/resource scope, authority, candidate and evidence references, pending gates and cumulative
repair history. On a substantive change, stop/settle the previous work, transfer ownership once,
and send only the new scope and necessary evidence. Keep rejected hypotheses and accepted units.

Source implementation and security-sensitive guards need an appropriate implementation owner.
Investigator does not become a writer by remaining available. Tester executes approved procedures
and setup; check design, new browser helpers, locator diagnosis and measurement methodology belong
to Main or appropriate implementation/diagnosis ownership, even under ignored artifact directories.
Reviewer does not grade its own repair or the adequacy of a test it silently changed.

When a native role/preset cannot be changed, use the correct named worker or let Main finish an
already-understood tiny change after transfer. Saying “you are now Senior” does not change a model
or override a named worker's instructions. Do not force a fresh worker for every coherent correction.
The optional work_contracts.py transition check preserves counters and gates and rejects a proposed
same-handle role relabel. It checks supplied facts, not actual authority or native role activation.

Return one handoff: candidate; changed deliverables; requirement/check outcomes and evidence paths;
remaining blocker or next action. Send an intermediate message only for a decision, meaningful new
evidence, resource conflict, genuine blocker, or requested status. No repeated no-change acknowledgements.
Main should not answer every progress message or split one coherent assignment into a stream of
microtasks. Keep important decisions visible; encryption or message counts cannot prove wasted work.

## Lifecycle and capacity are different facts

Distinguish created, queued, working, work-completed, retained/open, closing and closure-confirmed.
Completion and a saved transcript prove neither closure nor continuous native residency. A followup
may start a new turn on a retained worker; it is not proof of close/resume. Never invent queue delay.

Five is the Smart-owned open-worker ceiling for this task's child tree, excluding Main. Native
capacity may use a different documented domain or include Main. Resolve the actual backend's
available status/config/help at a needed boundary; multiple cap namespaces and legacy aliases are
not proof of precedence. Do not copy one setting into every namespace or change limits automatically.
The optional capacity operation normalizes an explicitly supplied effective-domain observation;
unknown/stale evidence does not establish spare slots. allocation.py still checks scopes/reservations.

Before reusing a capacity slot, preserve results and owned resources and use supported native
closure for completed owned agents, then observe release. At uncertainty reconcile once, reuse a
known suitable stopped owner or proceed serially within known capacity. Do not spawn to probe a cap,
close unrelated/active work or waive required independent review. Persistent uncertainty stays visible.
No daemon, resident scheduler or mandatory per-command ledger is introduced.

Reuse current native capacity/role evidence within its unchanged domain; reconcile after a
material lifecycle/configuration change, missing evidence or admission error, not every command.
Transfer advice retains owner identities, risk and authority references. New semantic review
needs a held candidate as well as an independent suitable owner and the existing allocator checks.
